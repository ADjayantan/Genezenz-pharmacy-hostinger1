Health insurance & your medicine bills

A plain-language guide to how insurance works for pharmacy purchases in India — what's covered, cashless vs reimbursement, and the paperwork you need to get your money back.

Quick note: this is general information, not financial or insurance advice. Cover varies by policy — always check your own policy document or ask your insurer or TPA.

Are medicines actually covered?

It depends on why you are buying them:

  • As part of a hospitalisation: medicines given during an admitted hospital stay, and often those in the days just before and after (pre- and post-hospitalisation), are usually claimable.
  • Under an OPD / outpatient benefit: some policies include an outpatient allowance that covers doctor visits and pharmacy bills up to a limit. Check whether yours does.
  • Routine over-the-counter buys: a strip of paracetamol with no related claim is generally not covered.

Cashless vs reimbursement

Cashless

The insurer settles directly with a network hospital's pharmacy. You pay little or nothing up front, but it only works at network providers and needs pre-approval.

Reimbursement

You pay first and claim the money back afterwards by submitting bills and documents. This is the usual route for a local pharmacy purchase.

Documents you'll usually need

  • The original itemised pharmacy bill (with medicine names, quantities, prices and GST).
  • The doctor's prescription matching those medicines.
  • For a hospitalisation claim: the discharge summary and other hospital paperwork.
  • The claim form and your policy / TPA details.

Keep the originals safe — most insurers ask for original bills for reimbursement, and a missing bill is the most common reason a claim gets held up.

Common questions

Are medicines covered by health insurance in India?

Medicines are usually covered when they are part of a hospitalisation claim, or under an OPD/outpatient benefit if your policy includes one. Everyday over-the-counter purchases without a related claim are generally not covered.

What documents do I need to claim medicine bills?

Typically the original itemised pharmacy bill, the doctor's prescription, and — for a hospitalisation claim — the discharge summary and other hospital documents. Keep originals; insurers usually require them for reimbursement.
